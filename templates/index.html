How the Expert System Works

Unlike opaque black-box machine learning models, CareerWise AI implements an interpretable expert system with complete causal traceability.

1. Knowledge Base (KB)

Stores declarative domain knowledge compiled from career counseling experts: formal production rules (IF-THEN), career competency benchmarks, and Certainty Factor (CF) parameters.

Declarative Knowledge
2. Dual Inference Engine

Executes Forward Chaining (data-driven deduction from student facts) and Backward Chaining (goal-driven eligibility diagnostic) using MYCIN combination algebra.

Forward + Backward
3. Explanation Facility

Crucial component in Expert Systems: answers WHY a career was recommended, HOW the score was derived step-by-step, and WHY NOT for unranked paths.

AIES Academic Viva & Theory Guide

Answer: Machine Learning models (like deep neural networks) operate as black boxes where decisions cannot be causally explained to a student. In educational guidance, trust, accountability, and explainability are paramount. An Expert System provides:
  1. Full Explainability: The Explanation Facility reveals exact rules, prerequisite checks, and mathematical score derivations.
  2. Deterministic Predictability: Domain heuristics are guaranteed without hallucination.
  3. Zero Cold-Start: Does not require tens of thousands of labeled historical student records to work reliably.

Answer:
  • Forward Chaining (Data-Driven): Starts with user-asserted facts (working memory) → matches antecedents of production rules → fires satisfied rules → derives top career recommendations.
  • Backward Chaining (Goal-Driven): Starts with a hypothesis (e.g. "Can this student qualify as an AI/ML Engineer?") → finds rules concluding that goal → checks if required subgoals/prerequisites are satisfied in working memory → diagnoses exact skill deficits and blockers.

Answer: We employ the classical MYCIN combination formula (Shortliffe & Buchanan):
CF_combined = CF_prior + CF_rule × (1 − CF_prior)
When multiple independent production rules provide supporting evidence for a career hypothesis, the cumulative Certainty Factor increases monotonically toward 1.0 (100% certainty), modeling human expert reasoning under uncertainty.
